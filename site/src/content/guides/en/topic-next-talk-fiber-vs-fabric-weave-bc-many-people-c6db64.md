## Understanding Fiber vs. Fabric in Apparel Sourcing

Distinguishing between fiber and fabric construction is fundamental for accurate apparel sourcing. Fiber refers to the raw material, such as cotton, polyester, silk, or wool. Fabric, on the other hand, describes the structure created by interlacing (weaving), interlocking (knitting), or bonding these fibers. A common point of confusion arises because many fabric names, like satin or velvet, describe a specific construction method or surface appearance, not the fiber content.

For example, satin is a weave characterized by long float threads on the surface, giving it a smooth, lustrous finish. This weave can be executed using silk fibers, polyester fibers, or even cotton fibers (where it is typically called sateen). Similarly, velvet is a pile fabric, meaning it has a dense, soft pile of cut loops. Historically, velvet was made from silk, but modern production frequently uses polyester or acetate to achieve a similar aesthetic at a different price point. Understanding this distinction is crucial for specifying materials correctly and managing cost expectations.

## Practical Steps for Specifying Materials

1.  **Identify Fiber Composition First:** Clearly state the desired fiber content (e.g., 100% cotton, 95% polyester / 5% spandex, 70% viscose / 30% linen). This dictates the material's inherent properties like breathability, stretch, and handfeel.
2.  **Specify Fabric Construction:** After fiber, define the fabric structure (e.g., plain weave, twill weave, jersey knit, interlock knit, satin weave, velvet pile). This determines the fabric's drape, texture, and strength.
3.  **Determine Fabric Weight (GSM):** Specify the fabric weight in GSM (grams per square meter). This is a critical metric for quality and cost. For instance, a basic t-shirt might use a 160-180 GSM jersey knit, while a heavy hoodie could be 300-350 GSM fleece. The "best cotton fabric gsm" for a summer dress might be 120-150 GSM, whereas a durable work shirt could require a "cotton fabric gsm 200" or higher. Understanding "fabric weight vs gsm" is non-negotiable.
4.  **Define Yarn Specifications (if applicable):** For woven fabrics, specify yarn count (e.g., 40s, 20s) and ply (e.g., single, two-ply). For knits, specify yarn type and gauge.
5.  **Detail Finishing Processes:** Include any desired finishes such as mercerization, brushing, peaching, anti-pilling, water-repellent, or anti-microbial treatments. These significantly impact the final product's performance and feel.

## Guangzhou Wholesale Reality: Shisanhang Market Example

In Guangzhou, particularly at markets like Shisanhang (十三行), understanding these distinctions is paramount. When sourcing, you will encounter a vast array of fabrics. For instance, if you are looking for a "velvet fabric gsm" of 200-250 for a dress, you need to specify both the fiber (e.g., 100% polyester) and the velvet construction.

Typical MOQs for custom fabric development in Guangzhou can range from 500 kg to 1000 kg, depending on the fiber and complexity. For ready-made fabrics, you might find lower MOQs, sometimes as low as 100 meters per color, especially for common blends.

For garment production, a common MOQ per style/color is 300-500 pieces. For a basic t-shirt made from 180 GSM 100% cotton jersey, the unit price at 300 pieces might range from $3.50-$5.00 USD, depending on print, trims, and finishing. For a more complex item like a polyester velvet dress with a "velvet fabric gsm" of 220, the unit price at 300 pieces could be $12.00-$18.00 USD. These prices are based on FOB Guangzhou terms.

Quality Control (QC) is typically conducted with an AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) of 2.5 for major defects and 4.0 for minor defects. This means that for every 100 units inspected, no more than 2.5 units can have a major defect, and no more than 4.0 units can have a minor defect. This standard applies to both fabric quality and garment construction.
