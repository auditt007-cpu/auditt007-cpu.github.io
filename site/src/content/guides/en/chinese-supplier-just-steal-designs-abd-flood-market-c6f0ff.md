The risk of a Chinese supplier stealing your designs and flooding the market is present, but manageable with proper precautions and due diligence. It's not an inevitable outcome. Many reputable factories prioritize long-term relationships and understand the value of intellectual property. The key is to select the right partners and implement protective measures from the outset.

## Mitigating Design Theft Risks

### Understanding the Landscape
Guangzhou's apparel market, particularly around areas like Shisanhang and Shahe, is highly competitive. While many factories are ethical, some smaller, less established operations might be tempted to replicate successful designs. This is particularly true for simple, high-volume items or designs that gain rapid popularity, especially within the fast-growing segments of chinese streetwear brands or chinese street fashion brands.

### Practical Steps to Protect Your Designs

1.  **Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) and Non-Circumvention Agreement (NCA):** Always have a legally binding NDA and NCA in place before sharing any design details. Ensure these documents are drafted by a lawyer familiar with Chinese intellectual property law and are translated into Mandarin. This provides a legal framework for recourse.
2.  **Strategic Design Disclosure:** Do not share your entire collection at once. Start with a few styles, build trust, and then gradually introduce more designs. Provide only the necessary information for sampling and production, avoiding excessive detail that isn't immediately required.
3.  **Factory Vetting and Audits:** Conduct thorough background checks on potential suppliers. Look for factories with a history of working with international brands, good references, and certifications. Physical factory audits are crucial to assess their operational transparency and security measures.
4.  **Intellectual Property Registration:** Register your designs, trademarks, and copyrights in China. While this can be a lengthy process, it provides the strongest legal protection against infringement. Without local registration, enforcement can be significantly more challenging.
5.  **Component Sourcing Control:** If possible, source unique components (e.g., custom zippers, specific trims, unique fabric blends) from a different supplier than your main garment manufacturer. This makes it harder for a factory to replicate the entire product without access to these specialized parts.
6.  **Regular Quality Control and Factory Visits:** Implement stringent quality control checkpoints throughout the production process. This not only ensures product quality but also allows for regular presence in the factory, which can deter unauthorized production. AQL 2.5 major / 4.0 minor is a standard benchmark for quality inspection in chinese manufacturing.

## Guangzhou Wholesale Reality and Numbers

In Guangzhou, the wholesale markets like Shisanhang are primarily for ready-made garments. For custom designs, you will work directly with factories. For a typical T-shirt, expect a fabric weight of 180-220 GSM for cotton jersey. For hoodies, 300-380 GSM fleece is common.

The minimum order quantity (MOQ) per style per color for custom production is typically 300 pieces. Some factories might accept 200 pieces for very simple styles, but this is less common and often comes with a higher unit price.

Unit prices for a basic custom T-shirt (180 GSM cotton, simple print) at an MOQ of 300 pieces would range from $3.50 to $5.00 USD per piece. For a custom hoodie (320 GSM fleece, simple embroidery), expect $12.00 to $18.00 USD per piece at the same MOQ. These prices are FOB (Free On Board) Guangzhou.

Quality control is critical. Most reputable factories will adhere to AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) standards. A common standard for apparel is AQL 2.5 major / 4.0 minor. This means that during a final inspection, if a random sample of garments is pulled, no more than 2.5% can have major defects (e.g., incorrect sizing, broken seams), and no more than 4.0% can have minor defects (e.g., loose threads, small stains). Implementing a third-party quality inspector in chinese factories is a wise investment to ensure these standards are met independently.
