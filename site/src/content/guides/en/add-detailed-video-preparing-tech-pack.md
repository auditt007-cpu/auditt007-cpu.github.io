Preparing a comprehensive tech pack is non-negotiable for efficient apparel sourcing in Guangzhou. Without a detailed tech pack, factories will quote inaccurately, production delays will occur, and quality issues are almost guaranteed. This document serves as the blueprint for your product, ensuring that what you envision is what gets produced.

## Practical Steps for Tech Pack Preparation

A robust tech pack streamlines communication and minimizes errors, especially when dealing with multiple suppliers across different markets like Shisanhang.

1.  **Cover Page and Bill of Materials (BOM):** Start with a clear cover page including style number, season, and product name. The BOM lists all components: fabric type, composition, weight (e.g., 180 GSM single jersey cotton), color codes (Pantone TPX/TCX), trims (buttons, zippers, labels), and their respective suppliers if specified.
2.  **Detailed Sketches and Construction:** Provide flat sketches (front, back, and side views) with all design details clearly visible. Include close-up sketches for intricate elements like pockets, collars, and cuffs. Specify all stitching types (e.g., single needle, double needle, overlock) and seam allowances.
3.  **Measurement Specifications (Spec Sheet):** This is critical. Include a graded spec sheet for all sizes, detailing point-of-measurements (POMs) with clear diagrams. For example, chest width (1" below armhole), body length (from HPS), sleeve length (from shoulder seam). Allowable tolerances (e.g., +/- 0.5 inches) must be stated.
4.  **Artwork and Print/Embroidery Details:** If your design includes graphics, provide high-resolution vector files (AI, EPS). Specify placement, size, color codes (Pantone), and application method (e.g., screen print, digital print, embroidery stitch count).
5.  **Labeling and Packaging Instructions:** Detail all required labels: main label, care label (with international symbols), size label, and any hangtags. Specify material, dimensions, and placement. For packaging, include polybag dimensions, carton markings, and packing ratios (e.g., 1:2:2:1 per size run).
6.  **Quality Control (QC) Standards:** Outline your acceptable quality levels. For instance, AQL 2.5 for major defects and AQL 4.0 for minor defects. Specify critical checkpoints: fabric inspection, in-line production checks, and final random inspection.

## Guangzhou Sourcing Realities

Guangzhou's apparel markets offer diverse options, but specific numbers are crucial for budgeting and planning. When we talk about are tech pack requirements, these figures become the foundation.

### Fabric and MOQ

For knitwear, such as basic T-shirts, a common fabric weight is 180 GSM 100% cotton single jersey. For woven items like shirts, 120 GSM poplin is typical. Denim often comes in 10oz (340 GSM) to 12oz (405 GSM) weights. Minimum Order Quantities (MOQ) vary by market and factory. In Shahe, for basic styles, you might find MOQs as low as 300 pieces per style per color. For more specialized fabrics or complex designs, especially in Shisanhang, MOQs typically range from 500 to 1000 pieces per style per color. Zhanxi, known for denim and casual wear, often requires 500-800 pieces for custom washes.

### Pricing and Quality Control

Unit pricing is directly tied to MOQ, fabric, and complexity. For a basic 180 GSM cotton T-shirt at 300 MOQ, expect prices between $3.50-$5.00 USD per piece. A simple woven blouse at 500 MOQ might be $7.00-$10.00 USD. For a denim jacket (12oz, 405 GSM) at 800 MOQ, prices could range from $18.00-$28.00 USD per piece. These prices are FOB Guangzhou.

Quality control is implemented with AQL 2.5 for major defects and AQL 4.0 for minor defects. This means that in a sample size, if the number of major defects exceeds 2.5% or minor defects exceeds 4.0%, the batch is rejected. Key QC checkpoints include:
*   **Fabric Inspection:** Before cutting, checking for color consistency, weaving defects, and weight.
*   **In-line Production Checks:** During sewing, verifying measurements, stitching quality, and print/embroidery accuracy.
*   **Final Random Inspection (FRI):** A comprehensive check of finished goods for overall quality, packaging, and labeling compliance before shipment.

A well-prepared tech pack, sometimes referred to as a tech pack 101, is the cornerstone of successful production. It ensures that all parties, from pattern makers to production managers, are working from the same precise instructions, minimizing misinterpretations and costly errors.
