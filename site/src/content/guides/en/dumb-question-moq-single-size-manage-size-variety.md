The Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ) is generally for a single style and a single color, not for a single size. You can typically distribute that MOQ across a standard size run. This is a common point of confusion for new buyers, but managing size variety within the MOQ is standard practice in Guangzhou's apparel manufacturing industry.

Factories here, especially those supplying markets like Shisanhang or Shahe, operate on production efficiencies. Cutting and sewing a single size is not cost-effective. They need a certain volume per size to optimize fabric utilization and labor.

## Understanding MOQ and Size Distribution

When a factory quotes an MOQ of, for example, 300 pieces for a T-shirt, it means 300 pieces of that specific T-shirt design in one chosen color. You would then specify your size breakdown within those 300 pieces. A common ratio might be 1:2:2:1 for S-M-L-XL, meaning 50 S, 100 M, 100 L, and 50 XL pieces.

For basic knit items like T-shirts (180-220 GSM single jersey), a typical MOQ is 300-500 pieces per style per color. Unit prices for this range would be around $3.50-$5.00 USD, depending on fabric composition and detailing. For woven items like basic shirts or trousers, MOQs can be higher, often 500-800 pieces per style per color, with unit prices ranging from $8.00-$15.00 USD.

## Practical Steps for Managing Size Variety

1.  **Define Your Target Size Ratio:** Before contacting factories, have a clear idea of your desired size breakdown (e.g., S-M-L-XL, 1:2:2:1 ratio). This demonstrates preparedness and helps factories provide accurate quotes.
2.  **Communicate Clearly with Suppliers:** Explicitly state your desired size breakdown when requesting a quote. Do not assume the factory will automatically offer a standard ratio.
3.  **Negotiate Size Minimums:** While the overall MOQ is for the style/color, some factories might have a *minimum per size* for efficient cutting. This could be 50 pieces per size. If your desired ratio results in fewer than this for a particular size, you may need to adjust your overall MOQ or ratio.
4.  **Consider Fabric Utilization:** Factories aim to minimize fabric waste. Your size ratio should ideally allow for efficient fabric cutting. A very wide range of sizes (e.g., XS to 5XL) in small quantities can lead to higher fabric waste and potentially higher unit costs. This is where understanding your fabric gsm size and how it impacts cutting efficiency becomes crucial.
5.  **Factor in QC and AQL:** Quality control (QC) checks are performed on the entire batch, not per size. Standard AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) for apparel is typically 2.5 for major defects and 4.0 for minor defects. Ensure your QC plan covers all sizes proportionally.

When dealing with a clothing manufacturer low MOQ, especially for complex garments, the impact of size variety on cost and lead time is more pronounced. Factories specializing in clothing manufacturer small MOQ orders, often found in areas like Zhanxi, are more flexible but typically charge higher unit prices to compensate for the inefficiencies of smaller runs.

## Fabric GSM and Its Role

The fabric gsm size (grams per square meter) is a critical factor. For example, a 280 GSM French Terry hoodie will have different cutting and sewing requirements than a 160 GSM cotton jersey T-shirt. Factories need to ensure they have enough fabric of the correct GSM and color to fulfill the entire order, including all sizes. If you're sourcing a specific fabric gsm cutter size for your patterns, ensure the factory can accommodate this without excessive waste.

## Conclusion

Managing size variety within an MOQ is a fundamental aspect of apparel sourcing in Guangzhou. By understanding that the MOQ applies to the style and color, and by clearly communicating your size breakdown, you can effectively plan your production. Always confirm the factory's minimums per size and consider the impact of your size ratio on fabric utilization and overall cost.
