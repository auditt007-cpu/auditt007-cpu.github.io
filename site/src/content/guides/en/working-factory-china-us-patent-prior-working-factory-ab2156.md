Having a patent prior to engaging a factory, whether in China or the US, is a critical step for protecting your intellectual property. Factories in China, like anywhere else, can and sometimes do reproduce designs for resale if not properly secured. This is not unique to China, but the scale and speed of manufacturing here make the risk more pronounced. Protection is your responsibility.

## Intellectual Property Protection in Apparel Sourcing

Securing your designs before production is non-negotiable. While a full patent can be costly and time-consuming, especially for apparel, other forms of protection are often more practical and effective for clothing designs. These include design patents, copyright, and robust non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) and manufacturing agreements. The goal is to establish clear ownership and deter unauthorized reproduction.

### Understanding IP Risks in China

The perception of rampant intellectual property theft in China is often exaggerated but not entirely unfounded. The risk is higher with factories that lack established reputations or those offering unusually low prices. Reputable factories understand the importance of IP protection for long-term business relationships. However, without formal protection, proving infringement and seeking recourse becomes significantly more challenging. This is where a strong legal framework and diligent quality control China manufacturing practices become essential.

## Practical Steps for IP Protection

1.  **File for Design Patents or Copyrights:** For unique apparel designs, consider filing for design patents in key markets (e.g., US, EU, China) or registering copyrights for original prints and graphic designs. This provides a legal basis for enforcement.
2.  **Implement Robust NDAs:** Before sharing any design details, ensure a comprehensive Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) is signed by the factory. This NDA should be enforceable under Chinese law and specify penalties for breaches.
3.  **Draft Detailed Manufacturing Agreements:** Your manufacturing agreement should explicitly state that all intellectual property rights for the product belong to you, the buyer. It should also include clauses prohibiting the factory from manufacturing or selling your designs to third parties.
4.  **Control Design Files and Samples:** Only provide necessary design files. Watermark digital designs. Keep track of all physical samples provided to the factory and ensure they are returned or accounted for.
5.  **Conduct Due Diligence on Factories:** Research potential factories thoroughly. Look for factories with a history of working with international brands and positive references regarding IP respect. A factory that is transparent about its operations and has clear internal quality check China procedures is a better choice.
6.  **Regular Quality Control and Factory Audits:** Implement stringent quality control China processes. Regular factory audits, including checks on production lines and inventory, can help detect unauthorized production. A third-party quality inspector China can be invaluable here.

## Sourcing Realities and Pitfalls

Guangzhou's apparel markets, such as Shisanhang and Shahe, operate on high volume and quick turnover. Factories here are accustomed to producing for various brands. For a basic cotton T-shirt (180 GSM), the MOQ is typically 500 pieces per style/color, with unit prices ranging from $3.50 to $5.00 USD. For a more complex item like a denim jacket (10oz denim, 340 GSM), MOQs might be 300 pieces per style/color, with unit prices from $15.00 to $25.00 USD. Quality control checkpoints typically follow AQL 2.5 major / 4.0 minor standards.
